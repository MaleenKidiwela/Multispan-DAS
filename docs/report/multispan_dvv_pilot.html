Noise and channel quality

Coherent energy lies only between 0.12 and 0.7 Hz, in the secondary microseism and dispersive Scholte waves. Above 0.7 Hz neighbouring channels 0.2 km apart are incoherent. Removing the common mode made no measurable difference.

A channel counts as physical when its median coherence with the channel two positions away is at least 0.15. The readme's examples on the south cable come out right: 258–260 km is bad, 261–263 and 265–268 km are good, and beyond 290 km is bad. Coherence is highest just past each repeater and decays along the span. The shallow first ~20 km is also incoherent, probably because slow ocean-wave energy is aliased at 212 m spacing.

Convergence

Each 10–120-min stack is compared with a reference built from all January runs except the one being tested, for single pairs and for 1, 2 and 5 km spatial stacks. Stacking nearby channels helps most for single pairs: on north span A1 at 30 min, CC rises from 0.74 for a single pair to 0.90 for a 5-km stack. The gain flattens beyond about 2 km.

dv/v precision and tides

dv/v is measured by stretching each 30-min stack (moving in 10-min steps) against the all-January reference. Results are averaged over offsets and both lag sides, weighted by Weaver et al. (2011) errors, and then over each span. The vectorised stretching matches noisepy to within 10⁻⁵ on synthetics. A windowed cross-correlation method agrees with it at r = 0.5–0.7 on the coda windows but only 0.2–0.4 on the short early-coda window.

An even/odd null test splits each hour into interleaved 10-min halves and measures each half separately. It gives an empirical floor with no bias (median difference ≈ 0).

No tidal signal is detected. Of 142 regressions on the NOAA South Beach prediction, 9 have |z| > 2, against about 6.5 expected by chance. Run-to-run offsets of ±0.2–0.4 % often differ in sign between bands, which points to changes in the noise sources rather than in velocity. There is no step at the Jan 21 acquisition change. The south-cable floors rest on only 9 null-test samples per span and are less reliable.

Next step: the continuous archive

  • Process the 3.9 TB continuous record at piweb.ooirsn.uw.edu/das25 in periods with constant acquisition settings. 211.7 m spacing runs from Jan 6 to Jan 28, and the sampling rate changes on Jan 13 and Jan 21.
  • Keep the first ~20–40 km past each repeater, 0.12–0.7 Hz, offsets of 0.4–3 km, and 2–5 km spatial stacks.
  • Day-long stacks should make the late coda usable. Treat direct-wave and early-coda dv/v as sensitive to changes in the noise sources.
  • If the noise is white, ~3 months of data would bring tidal sensitivity down to ~3–7 × 10⁻⁵ per m. That approaches plausible tidal-loading signals. This is a rough extrapolation.
